# Spread is an opt-in column around mid prices

A backtest of a forex or intraday strategy is wrong without trading costs, and MarketForge published none. We now simulate a quoted bid-ask spread and publish it as one optional `spread` column, in price units, next to candles whose prices are defined as mid prices (bid = price - spread / 2). We rejected separate bid and ask candle files: they double the output and force every user to choose a side, while a mid price and a spread reconstruct both. We rejected redefining the existing prices as bid prices (the MetaTrader convention): it would have changed the meaning of every dataset already generated.

The spread is opt-in and draws from its own random stream (`(seed, market, day, stream 2)`), so requesting it changes no other number and the feature did not need a seed-breaking release (ADR 0007). On higher timeframes a candle carries the mean spread of its minutes.

Per minute the spread is the instrument's typical level times a power of the turbulence, a power of the inverse of intraday seasonality, a term that decays over the first minutes after a closure, and lognormal noise, floored at one tick for stocks. It has no memory of its own: all persistence comes from turbulence, which keeps the simulator's checkpoint unchanged (ADR 0009).

## Consequences

- **Crypto spreads are fitted to measured quotes.** Binance USDT-futures best bid/ask for seven coins (February 2024) show spreads sitting on the exchange's tick: 0.024 basis points on BTC, 1 to 2 on coins under a dollar, and only 0 to 35% wider in the most turbulent minutes. Our first estimate (1.5 basis points on BTC, widening with the square root of turbulence) was about fifty times too wide and far too reactive. The crypto level is now a power law of the price, quoted as a fixed price increment that does not follow the price down, with a turbulence power of 0.1, and the crypto spread bands are anchored to those measurements (ADR 0006). Four coins were measured over the usual two 14-day windows; BTC, ETH and SOL over one day each, because their archives are 3 to 7 GB a month each.
- **Forex and stock levels are estimates.** No free source of forex or stock quotes was reachable. Built-in instruments get their typical level from a per-market rule on their volatility (more volatile means less liquid), not from per-instrument research, and users set `spread_bps` to match their broker. The README says so wherever a number appears.
- **The forex and stock spread bands are provisional** and only rule out the absurd: a median outside 0.05 to 40 basis points, or the most turbulent minutes being more than 10% narrower than the median. That floor was first 0.95 and was lowered to 0.9 without real-data evidence, after one generated EURPLN fortnight measured 0.935: its most turbulent minutes were the liquid hours, when spreads are tightest. Measuring real quotes replaces both bands.
- A fixed price increment means a crypto spread in basis points drifts as the price does over a long range, as it does on an exchange that leaves its tick unchanged.
- Spread does not model commissions, slippage or depth. A strategy that trades size still needs its own cost model.
